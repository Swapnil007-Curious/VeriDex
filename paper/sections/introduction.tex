\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Language models state fluent claims that their inputs do not support \citep{ji2023survey}.
Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) grounds an answer in retrieved passages \citep{lewis2020rag,karpukhin2020dpr}, but grounding is not the same as support: generative search engines often cite passages that do not back the sentence they are attached to \citep{liu2023verifiability}, and commercial legal research tools built on retrieval still hallucinate \citep{magesh2024hallucination,dahl2024fictions}.
For statutes the cost of an unsupported sentence is concrete: a wrong time limit or monetary threshold misleads a reader who cannot easily check the source.

We ask two questions about the small domain collections that individual practitioners build.
Which design choices change what the generator actually sees, given that chunkers are usually compared by Recall@$k$, which ignores how many retrieved chunks fit into the generator's fixed token budget?
And what does verification buy and cost, given that withholding answers raises the faithfulness of what is released but fails users on answerable questions, and that a check sharing its criterion with the metric can look better than it is?

We study these questions with VeriDex (codename \emph{agentic-rag}), an open pipeline evaluated on Indian consumer-protection law (the Consumer Protection Act, 2019 and its Rules; \V{cl.docs} documents) and on selected chapters of \V{cs.docs} open computer-science textbooks.
It runs on a \V{hw.cores}-core laptop CPU with a small local generator (qwen2.5:3b); a hosted open-weight model serves as a generator-scale ablation. All tuning of system parameters used a dev split; test access was logged.

Our contributions (the first three rest on test-split results):
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Structure-aware chunking measured by token-budget coverage.} Chunks that follow sections and clauses raise the coverage of gold provisions within a \V{gen.budget}-token context on the statutes (\V{cl.ret.struct-b.dcov} \V{cl.ret.struct-b.dcovci}; \V{cl.strict.strict.d} under a strict relevance rule), while the Recall@5 difference is \V{cl.ret.struct-b.dr5sig}. Fixed-256 hybrid retrieval with reranking comes close, far more slowly, so short chunks may explain the gain as much as section alignment; on the textbooks neither difference is significant (\S\ref{sec:res-retrieval}).
\item \textbf{An accounting of verification.} Citation and number guards plus a critic raise released-answer faithfulness on the statutes (\V{cl.rel.G4-G2} \V{cl.rel.G4-G2ci}, significant after Holm correction), but almost entirely by withholding answers with uncited sentences: NLI support of cited sentences changes by \V{cl.cit.G4-G2} \V{cl.cit.G4-G2ci}, and the critic with its revision round adds nothing measurable over the guards used as a filter. A retrieval-score threshold tuned on dev lowers accuracy (\S\ref{sec:res-generation}, \S\ref{sec:res-abstention}).
\item \textbf{A generator-scale ablation and an error analysis.} \gptoss{} (with larger budgets) in place of the local generator changes accuracy by \V{cl.boot.G2H-G2.acc} \V{cl.boot.G2H-G2.accci} on the statutes (inconclusive) and by \V{cs.boot.G2H-G2.acc} \V{cs.boot.G2H-G2.accci} on the textbooks (permutation $p=\V{sec.cs.G2H-G2.acc.p}$, uncorrected); \V{err.evalartifact} of \V{err.n} sampled failures are artefacts of the automatic evaluation (\S\ref{sec:res-generation}, \S\ref{sec:res-errors}).
\item \textbf{A deployment without hosting costs.} A static site searches \V{lib.passages} passages in the browser from a \V{lib.vecgz}\,MB int8 index. We report load and retrieval latency (on dev questions) and list how this pipeline differs from the evaluated one; its answer quality is not measured (\S\ref{sec:deploy}).
\end{enumerate}
The code, configurations, result tables and the scripts that generate every number in this paper are public.\footnote{\url{https://github.com/Swapnil007-Curious/agentic-rag}}
